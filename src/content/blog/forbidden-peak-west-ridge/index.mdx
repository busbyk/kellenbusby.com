## Key Beta

- Car to car was really nice but I wouldn't hate spending a night at the Boston Basin high camp
- There's water everywhere. You don't need to carry much for most of the approach. But I did bring a liter and a half on route from the last fill up before we crossed snow.
- This time of year we probably could have avoided snow completely. Definitely didn't need axe and crampons.
- Simuling is the way to go
- We all just wore approach shoes and felt secure
- The cat scratch gullys are indeed chossy. It was nice to simul the first part of them and then unrope for the top bit. Try to descend without anyone above you - lots of small rocks to kick loose.
- A single rack from .3 - 2 and some nuts with 10 alpines was a perfect rack for us
- Apparently, you should check for bridge closures before going down Cascade River Rd

## The return of the bonk bois

Sean's been out for big adventures, rebuilding knee #3. Turns out he's been doing a great job at PT cuz it survived a proper day in the mountains.

Originally, it was Addison and Mel's idea to climb forbidden and Sean and I would join them. They've been eyeing it up for a while but sadly Mel had a bad crash on the mountain bike on Saturday. I look forward to their glorious summit photo someday.

Weather was bad on Saturday so we pivoted to a Monday ascent which is, honestly, my preference. Week days are clutch for classics. When Matt heard we could re-unite the bonk bois, he finagled a day off work.

We opted for car to car. Hiking up the Boston Basin trail with a light pack is _so_ much better than with an overnight pack.

## Approach to the Cat Scratch Gullys

After high camp is a sea of slabs. Earlier in the summer, you'd end up spending a decent bit of time on snow but we really only had to cross a small patch of low angle snow. I love slabs like these. It's such cool terrain to be in. The features the water has carved, darker colored dikes in the rock, streaks, mini ridges, and just fun little features to play on the whole way. And the scale is just cool.

## The real tax

Sometimes you learn a new way to epic. And after a stellar, smooth day, we thought we were in the clear.

When we'd gotten to the summit and ran into those other parties, they'd told us that the Cascade River Bridge was going to be closed from 8pm to 4am that night. We knew we weren't going to make it there by 8pm so that was problematic.

We were in disbelief about this because how can you close a bridge where there is no other way across and is where many full day mountain adventures happen on a daily basis?

Matt was able to get an inReach text out to his wife, Elsa and she told us that there was another road that we could take to get to a different bridge to get across. Perfect! So we descended thinking our adventure tax would simply be a long, dirt road.

Welp, turns out that road got washed out this December along with the rest of Washington.

So we came to the very sad realization that we would be sleeping three very tired, very sweaty dudes in Matt's Subaru until 4am. We feel a little wronged by Skagit County for not putting a sign up when we crossed that morning (we swear there was no sign telling us the bridge would be closed). But, owell.
